## Planned Sections

1. **Loss Aversion** — Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory (1979); losses hurt approximately 2× as much as equivalent gains feel good; how this manifests in holding losing stocks too long and refusing to sell underperforming properties at a loss
2. **Anchoring Bias** — Fixating on purchase price or past highs; "I'll sell when it gets back to what I paid" in stocks; "My house was worth $X in 2021" in real estate
3. **Herd Mentality** — FOMO in stock rallies (meme stocks, tech bubbles); FOMO in real estate booms (bidding wars, waiving inspections); social pressure to follow the crowd
4. **Confirmation Bias** — Seeking information that supports existing beliefs; stock enthusiasts reading only stock-bullish content; real estate enthusiasts consuming only RE success stories
5. **Endowment Effect** — Overvaluing assets simply because of ownership; particularly strong in real estate (emotional attachment to property); less visceral with stock shares
6. **Recency Bias** — Assuming recent performance predicts future results; after a bull market, both assets "always go up"; after a crash, both are "too risky"
7. **Analysis Paralysis** — Overthinking prevents action; comparing 50 properties without buying; researching 200 stocks without investing; the cost of inaction
8. **Sunk Cost Fallacy** — Continuing to hold or invest in a losing position because of what has already been spent; pouring money into a bad rental property because of renovation costs already incurred
9. **How Each Bias Manifests Differently** — Side-by-side table showing how the same cognitive bias plays out in stocks vs. real estate
10. **Strategies for Mitigating Psychological Traps** — Rules-based investing (predetermined buy/sell criteria), automation (dollar-cost averaging, auto-rebalancing), checklists (pre-investment criteria), accountability (advisor, partner, peer group)
