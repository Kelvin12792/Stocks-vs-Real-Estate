## Reader Outcome

After reading this book, the reader should be able to:
- Understand how stocks and real estate work as investment vehicles
- Compare the two across eight critical dimensions
- Identify which asset class (or combination) aligns with their personal financial profile
- Apply decision-making frameworks to their own situation
- Recognize and avoid common psychological traps and myths

---

## Content Architecture

### Comparison Structure
- Each concept compares both asset classes **side-by-side within the same chapter**
- Final chapters serve as a creative, multi-format decision guide

### Eight Comparison Dimensions (woven through Part II)
1. Returns and growth potential
2. Risk and volatility
3. Cash flow and passive income
4. Time and effort required
5. Tax advantages
6. Liquidity and flexibility
7. Leverage and financing
8. Barrier to entry (capital needed)

---

## Content Features

### Per Chapter
- Opens with a relevant quote (investors, philosophers, thinkers)
- Inline jargon definitions on first use
- At least one side-by-side comparison table
- At least one case study (fictional persona or real event)
- All data sourced with inline citations
- Key takeaways at chapter end (4–6 items, no exercises)

### Dedicated Sections
| Topic | Treatment |
|-------|-----------|
| Behavioral psychology and emotional mistakes | Full chapter (Ch. 14) |
| Tax/legal (capital gains, 1031 exchanges, structures) | Full chapter (Ch. 9) |
| Myths and misconceptions | Standalone chapter (Ch. 13) |
| Future trends (AI, tokenization, algorithmic trading) | Full chapter (Ch. 16) |
| Combined portfolio strategy | Full chapter (Ch. 15) |
| Modern alternatives (REITs, fractional, robo-advisors) | Mentioned inline, not full chapters |
| Personal financial readiness | Acknowledged lightly, not a core framework |

### Decision Framework (Chapter 17)
Three tools provided to the reader:
1. **Scoring model** — Rate yourself on key factors, tally the result
2. **Decision tree** — Answer yes/no questions, follow the path
3. **Persona matching** — Find the investor profile that most resembles your situation

## Sources and Credibility

| Element | Approach |
|---------|----------|
| **Famous investors** | Warren Buffett, Benjamin Graham, Ray Dalio, Robert Kiyosaki, John Bogle, and others |
| **Lesser-known voices** | Credible but non-celebrity investors, researchers, and practitioners |
| **Principles** | Investment principles presented independently of personalities |
| **Philosophers** | Quotes from thinkers (Seneca, Aristotle, Nassim Taleb, etc.) to open chapters |
| **Case studies** | Both fictional personas AND real historical events (2008 crash, dot-com bubble, etc.) |
| **Data** | Digestible — tables and simple comparisons, no heavy charts |
| **Citations** | Inline only — no footnotes, no endnotes, no bibliography |

## Glossary Strategy

- **Inline definitions:** Bold the term, define in parenthetical on first use
- **Glossary appendix:** Full alphabetical glossary in back matter
- **Dual-layer approach** ensures accessibility for both casual readers and those using the book as reference

## Workflow

1. **Planning phase** — Complete ✅
2. **Outline approval** — Full outline in `planning/OUTLINE.md`
3. **Chapter drafting** — Author selects chapters to draft; each chapter follows `templates/CHAPTER_TEMPLATE.md`
4. **Review and verification** — Each chapter checked against fact check and legal review trackers
5. **Final assembly** — All chapters compiled into complete manuscript

---

## Research Integrity Standards

1. **No hallucinated data** — Every number, percentage, and statistic must come from a verifiable, published source
2. **No assumed regulations** — All tax rules, legal structures, and regulatory claims must reference specific laws, IRS codes, SEC rules, or regulatory bodies with verification dates
3. **All calculations reproducible** — Every ROI, compound growth, or comparison calculation must show its formula and inputs
4. **Source dating** — Every factual claim must note when the source was published so future editions can flag outdated information
5. **Living audit trail** — `FACT_CHECK.md` and `LEGAL_REVIEW.md` serve as verification logs across editions
