# Myths List

## Purpose
Complete inventory of myths and misconceptions to be addressed in the book. Primary home is Chapter 13, but myths are also referenced in relevant comparison chapters throughout Part II.

| M1 | "Real estate always goes up" | Real Estate | National home prices fell ~27% from peak to trough during 2006–2012. Many local markets have experienced decades of stagnation. | S&P/Case-Shiller Index; FRED | Ch. 13 | Ch. 5, 6 |

| M5 | "Real estate is the safest investment" | Real Estate | Real estate carries concentration risk (single asset, single location), leverage risk (mortgage amplifies losses), illiquidity risk, and maintenance/liability risk. | 2008 housing crisis data; FDIC bank failure data tied to RE loans | Ch. 13 | Ch. 6 |
| M6 | "Stocks are too volatile for serious investors" | Stocks | Volatility (short-term price fluctuation) is not the same as risk (permanent loss of capital). Long-term diversified stock investors have historically been compensated for enduring short-term volatility. | Ibbotson SBBI data; Dimson, Marsh & Staunton, *Triumph of the Optimists* | Ch. 13 | Ch. 6 |
| M7 | "Landlording is easy passive income" | Real Estate | Property management involves tenant screening, maintenance coordination, legal compliance, bookkeeping, and emergency response. The average landlord spends significant time or pays 8–12% of gross rent to a manager. | NARPM surveys; BiggerPockets landlord surveys | Ch. 13 | Ch. 7, 8 |
| M8 | "You can't lose money in an index fund over 20 years" | Stocks | While the US market has delivered positive 20-year returns historically, this is not guaranteed. Sequence of returns risk, international market examples (Japan's Nikkei: 1989–2009), and inflation erosion can result in poor real outcomes. | Nikkei 225 historical data; sequence of returns research | Ch. 13 | Ch. 5, 6 |
| M9 | "Location, location, location is all that matters in real estate" | Real Estate | Location is important but not sufficient. Purchase price, financing terms, property condition, management quality, tenant selection, and market timing all significantly affect returns. | Academic RE investment studies; case studies of overpriced properties in "good" locations | Ch. 13 | Ch. 3, 5 |
| M10 | "Timing the market is the key to stock success" | Stocks | Studies consistently show that time in the market outperforms market timing. Missing just the 10 best trading days over a 20-year period can cut returns by more than half. | JP Morgan Asset Management studies; Dalbar QAIB | Ch. 13 | Ch. 5, 14 |
| M11 | "You should pay off your mortgage as fast as possible" | Real Estate | If the mortgage rate is lower than expected investment returns, the mathematical advantage favors investing the difference. However, this depends on risk tolerance and guaranteed vs. expected returns. | Comparative analysis: mortgage rate vs. historical stock returns | Ch. 13 | Ch. 11 |
| M12 | "REITs are the same as owning real estate" | Real Estate | REITs trade like stocks, have stock-like volatility, and offer no direct control. They provide real estate exposure but behave differently from direct property ownership in terms of tax treatment, leverage, and management. | NAREIT data; academic correlation studies | Ch. 13 | Ch. 3, 10 |
| M13 | "You need a lot of knowledge to invest in stocks" | Stocks | Index fund investing requires minimal specialized knowledge. A single total-market fund provides diversified exposure. The knowledge barrier is primarily a barrier to active stock picking, not to equity participation. | Bogle, *The Little Book of Common Sense Investing* | Ch. 13 | Ch. 2, 12 |
| M14 | "Real estate provides better tax benefits than stocks" | Both | Both asset classes offer significant but different tax advantages. Stocks benefit from long-term capital gains rates, tax-loss harvesting, and tax-advantaged accounts. Real estate benefits from depreciation, 1031 exchanges, and mortgage interest deductions. The "better" tax treatment depends on the individual's specific situation. | IRS publications; comparative tax analysis | Ch. 13 | Ch. 9 |

## Myth-Busting Format (Chapter 13)

Each myth in Chapter 13 follows this structure:

```
### Myth #[X]: "[Myth statement]"

**Why people believe it:**
[2–3 sentences on why this belief is common and intuitively appealing]

**The reality:**
[3–5 sentences presenting the evidence-based truth with specific data and sources]

**The takeaway:**
[1–2 sentences distilling the key lesson]
```

## Cross-Reference Protocol

When a myth is relevant to a Part II chapter, it should be briefly mentioned with a forward reference:
```
This belief — that real estate values only move in one direction — is addressed in detail in Chapter 13. For now, the data tells a more nuanced story.
```

This avoids duplicating content while building anticipation for the myths chapter.
