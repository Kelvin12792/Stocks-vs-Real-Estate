### Chapter 14 — The Psychology of Investing: Why Smart People Make Bad Decisions
**Target: 3,500 words**

**Opening quote theme:** Self-knowledge, discipline, and the mind

**Key topics:**
- Loss aversion: Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory — losses hurt twice as much as equivalent gains
- Anchoring bias: fixating on purchase price or past highs
- Herd mentality: FOMO in stock rallies and real estate booms
- Confirmation bias: seeking information that supports existing beliefs about stocks or real estate
- Endowment effect: overvaluing assets simply because of ownership (especially real estate)
- Recency bias: assuming recent performance predicts future results
- Analysis paralysis: overthinking prevents action in both asset classes
- Sunk cost fallacy: holding losing investments too long
- How each bias manifests differently in stocks vs. real estate
- Strategies for mitigating psychological traps: rules-based investing, automation, checklists, accountability
- Side-by-side table: how each bias shows up in stocks vs. real estate

**Data sources needed:** Kahneman & Tversky (1979), Thaler's behavioral economics work, Dalbar's Quantitative Analysis of Investor Behavior
